About Signoff

Dovetail Signoff

The design review tools Dovetail engineers use on every project. Each review becomes a structured, traceable record: what was checked, what was found, who resolved it, and whether the design meets its requirements.

930 electrical checks845 mechanical checks28 fabrication DFM rules

How a review works

  1. Describe the design

    Pick the checklists and describe the product: battery-powered, mains, RF, high-speed, injection molded, sealed, handheld… Checks for features the design doesn't have are set aside, so the review fits the product instead of a generic list.

  2. Review every check

    Each check carries guidance on what to look for. Reviewers mark it PassIssueN/A and note what they found.

  3. Track issues to closure

    Every issue has a severity, an owner, a reference to the design, a discussion, and an outcome: open, fixed or waived. Nothing is left in someone's inbox.

  4. Trace requirements

    List what the product must do, how each requirement will be verified (inspection, analysis, demonstration or test), and which checks show it's met. A requirement whose checks have open issues is flagged.

  5. Sign off

    The review report collects the summary, every finding and the requirements compliance matrix, with sign-off lines for reviewer and approver.

Built for real collaboration

Several engineers can review the same design at once. Their work merges record by record, so no one overwrites anyone.

Access you control

Reviews are kept in private, access-controlled storage. Each person sees only the projects they've been given.

One family of tools

The tools share one design, with light and dark mode, and link to each other, so moving from an electrical review to a fab check is one click.

Grounded checklists

Checklists are written by Dovetail engineers. Fabrication limits are drawn from IPC standards and published board-house capabilities, with sources recorded.

Fabrication

Fabcheck

Fabcheck checks a PCB fabrication and assembly package the way a board house and an assembler would, before you send it. It reads the Gerbers, drill files, pick-and-place, BOM, IPC-356 netlist and ODB++, then shows what's wrong and exactly where on the board.

Files never leave the computer. Every package is processed inside the browser; nothing is uploaded.
AreaExamples
PackageMissing or extra layers, units, board outline, drill report, layer count against the stackup
AssemblyPick-and-place against BOM against copper: designators, do-not-place parts, quantities, parts on pads, rotation, pin 1
BOMMissing part numbers, duplicate designators, values or footprints that disagree, manufacturer and part-type mismatches
Fabrication DFMTrace width and spacing, annular ring, drill size and aspect ratio, hole spacing, copper to edge, slivers, solder mask dams, silkscreen on pads, via-in-pad, BGA pads, outline corners, copper balance

Rules for your board house. Start from typical limits (Prototype, Standard or Advanced HDI-lite, each with documented sources), or set the limits to a specific fab's published capabilities. Change a limit and the board is re-checked straight away; click any finding and the board view zooms to it. Two revisions of a package can also be compared side by side.
