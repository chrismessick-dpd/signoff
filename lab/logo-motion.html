Logo motion lab

The Dovetail mark is a block drawn in perspective with a dovetail set into it. The first two tabs animate it different ways; the Loom tab animates Loom's own mark. This is a prototype. Nothing here is used by the tools yet.

Your system asks for reduced motion, so the marks start still. Press Play to watch them anyway.

Settings

The dovetail sits in the block, is tapped out of its right side a tap at a time, falls away to leave the logo, and then seats again. With cutting on, each round starts from a blank block and the dovetail's lines are drawn in first.

Cutting time is how long the lines take to draw in. Seated is how long the dovetail sits in the block before the taps start. Tap-out speed sets how quickly the taps come and the dovetail falls. Logo shown is how long the logo stays before the dovetail seats again.

The block spins half a turn about z, into the image, while the dovetail stays put. Then the dovetail spins about y, up the image, changing color each turn. It rests as the logo between moves.

Loom's mark is a twisted pair. Here the two strands spin about the axis they share, then rest as the mark. The strands are alike, so every half turn lands back on the mark. The strand in front cuts a gap through the one behind, so one passes over the other; while it spins, the strand behind is also a little fainter.

Size is how much of the tile's width the strands span (the Dovetail mark is 60% of its tile's height). Weight is the stroke in the mark's 20-unit drawing; Loom's sidebar uses 1.7. Set the rest to 0 to spin without stopping.

In the tools, only the sidebar mark would move, and it would stay still for anyone whose system asks for reduced motion.
